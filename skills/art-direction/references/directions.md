# Directions

Twelve compositions to choose from in step 4. Each is a way of arranging a page and a temperament,
independent of colour. Shortlist three that could carry an item from the subject inventory, pick one,
and write one line on why. Mixing two is allowed when one leads and the other supports.

| # | Direction | Composition | Suits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Poster** | One huge typographic statement per viewport, image as a second layer, very few elements | Events, launches, manifestos, campaigns | Needs a sentence worth shouting; empty without one |
| 2 | **Object study** | The product or artefact large and central, details called out around it like a technical plate | Physical products, watches, instruments, food, furniture | Requires excellent imagery or a drawn object |
| 3 | **Editorial index** | Strong left-aligned grid, numbered or dated entries, captions doing real work | Studios, archives, journals, menus, collections | Can drift into broadsheet pastiche; keep it purposeful |
| 4 | **Field and figure** | Full-bleed colour fields per section, one figure (photo, product, illustration) on each | Brands with a strong colour, hospitality, consumer goods | Palette must be built for it; contrast on every field |
| 5 | **Live instrument** | The real interface or a working demo is the hero; the page explains around it | Software, tools, APIs, data products | Must use real-looking data; never grey placeholders |
| 6 | **Place** | The location leads: map fragments, street photography, local signage and materials | Restaurants, venues, hotels, local businesses, tourism | Needs real place imagery or open-archive pictures |
| 7 | **Timeline / process** | The page follows a real sequence (a day, a recipe, a build, a season) | Craft, manufacturing, roasters, bakeries, services with steps | Only when the content truly is a sequence |
| 8 | **Specimen** | Typography and its details are the visual system; large letterforms, sample sentences | Type foundries, publishers, agencies, writers | Body copy must stay readable inside the showpiece |
| 9 | **Gallery wall** | Work first, generous whitespace, minimal chrome, quiet captions | Portfolios, photographers, artists, architects | Navigation and case-study depth must still exist |
| 10 | **Scroll film** | Scroll is the timeline: pinned scenes, scrubbed video or image sequences, chapters | Products with a story, launches, immersive brand pieces | Performance, mobile fallback and reduced motion are mandatory (see `motion`) |
| 11 | **Document** | The page looks like the subject's own printed matter: a ticket, a label, a receipt, a field guide, a programme | Small brands with physical artefacts, events, festivals | Keep it a web page: real links, responsive, accessible |
| 12 | **Quiet utility** | Calm, dense, exact; information first, brand in details (type, one colour, icons) | B2B, developer tools, finance, healthcare | Must still have one signature detail or it is generic |

## Tone modifiers

Apply at most one, and only when the brief asks for it: **quiet, bold, warm, technical, playful,
luxurious, editorial**. A tone changes spacing, scale contrast, colour saturation and motion character;
it does not change the direction.

## Pairing a direction with the inventory

Write the pairing as a sentence before building:

> Direction 7 (timeline) carried by inventory item "the roaster lights the drum at 05:40": the page is
> one roasting day, from 05:40 to dispatch at 14:00, each section a timestamp.

If you cannot write that sentence for a direction, it is the wrong direction.
