# Copy

Words are design content. They exist to make the page understood and used, and a templated sentence
makes a page feel as generated as a templated layout.

## The page as an argument

List each section as a claim and its proof before writing anything else (see the SKILL file). Then
write each section to prove its claim and nothing else. If a sentence does not help prove the claim,
cut it.

## Headlines

- Say what it is or what changes for the reader, in their words. "Coffee roasted this morning, at your
  door tomorrow" beats "Elevate your coffee ritual".
- Specific beats clever. A number, a place, a time or a name is almost always stronger than an
  adjective.
- One idea per headline. If it needs "and", it may be two sections.
- Sentence case. No full stop on a one-line headline unless it is a deliberate voice choice.

## Body

- Short paragraphs, plain verbs, active voice.
- Concrete nouns from the subject inventory: the farm's name, the street, the material, the hour.
- No filler intensifiers (truly, really, incredibly, seamlessly).
- Claims the brief does not support are not written. No invented customers, awards, numbers or quotes.
  If social proof is needed and missing, leave a clearly marked slot and say so in the hand-off.

## Calls to action

- Say exactly what happens: "Book a table for tonight", "Start a 14-day trial", "Download the PDF
  (2 MB)".
- Keep the same verb through the flow: the button "Subscribe" produces "Subscribed", not "Success!".
- One primary action per viewport; secondary actions look secondary.

## Interface microcopy on pages

- Labels name things the way users think of them ("Delivery address", not "Shipping entity").
- Errors say what happened and how to fix it, without apologising and without blaming.
- Empty states invite the next action.
- Avoid icon-only controls unless the icon is universal; give every icon button an accessible name.

## Voice

Match the subject. A bakery can be warm and a little funny; a pension fund should be calm and exact.
Write two sample sentences in the plan to fix the voice before writing the page.

## Italian and other languages

Keep the user's language for the page copy. Typographic conventions change by language: Italian uses
« » or “ ” quotes, a space-free apostrophe (l’ordine), and comma decimals (1,5 kg). Set `lang` on the
`<html>` element so hyphenation and screen readers behave.
